## One service, three parts

**Rails.** We connect merchants to local acquiring in Latin America with a local commercial policy: local prices, local payment methods (cards, instant payments such as PIX, local instruments) and the best approval possible. This is where the capacity of a client's checkout actually grows: a market it could not reach alone becomes a market it can price for. We integrate several acquirers per market, side by side, so volume scales horizontally and no single rail limits us. We hold the relationship with every local entity, acquirer, bank and FX partner, so the merchant does not have to.

**Storefront.** Through the Website Factory we build the merchant's online store from templates, with our integrated acquirers as the checkout. We move the merchant from offline to online: we run the digital channel and the site, the merchant runs fulfillment. Post office for now; a shipping plan later. Open: how much weight the storefront carries in the strategy.

**Settlement.** Money collected locally reaches PaySecure with the fewest hops, by one of two paths. The partner path: local PIX, then the partner's tokenized-PIX rail, then the PaySecure balance. The on-ramp path we ran as A55 Payments: same-day PIX to an OTC desk, USDC into our own wallet, USDC on to PaySecure, at a cost of a few hundredths of a percent against PTAX. Open: the remittance contract and which path carries which volume.

## The PaySecure deal

SambaPay is the rebrand of A55 Payments and part of PaySecure (paysecure.net), a global payments platform. The agreement is simple: PaySecure brings the volume; SambaPay is its market enabler in Latin America and opens the local rails. Market enablement is the service we owe them, and the three parts above are how we deliver it. Brazil first, Mexico and Colombia next. Pricing and features are a separate conversation from volume.

## What we do not do

We do not do the merchant's fulfillment or shipping. We do not do anything that is not connecting merchants to local payment rails and taking care of the interfaces with the local entities. Only this, nothing beyond this.

## The one obsession

Respond as fast as possible to every interface need between our clients and the local entities where we have a regulated, PCI-compliant presence. Speed here is the product. A client that never gets a surprise from a local market it does not understand is a client that stays.
